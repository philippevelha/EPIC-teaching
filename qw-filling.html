What to watch

  • The carrier density per unit volume is held fixed, so the sheet density is n·L.
  • As L shrinks, e1 rises as roughly 1/L². The bulk states between the band edge and e1 are gone, and the carriers sit higher: compare the two Fermi levels and the mean energies.
  • Switch to Infinite walls and N(E): the staircase touches √E exactly at each corner, and the shaded gap is the state deficit (the Weyl surface term). States are moved up, never added.
  • The step height m*/(πħ²L) grows as 1/L, so just above e1 there are more states than bulk has just above its own edge. That is the useful sense in which the DOS "increases".

Model

  • Single-band effective mass, BenDaniel–Duke matching, spin degeneracy 2. Subbands use the GaAs in-plane mass; the DOS is normalised by the well width L.
  • Band offsets: ΔEg = 1.247x eV, split 65:35 between conduction and valence band. Masses: me = 0.067 + 0.083x, mhh = 0.51 + 0.25x (isotropic; the real heavy-hole in-plane mass is lighter). Light holes are not included.
  • Only bound states are populated. Carriers that would sit in the barrier continuum are not modelled; a warning appears when the Fermi level gets close to the barrier top.
  • No self-consistent Poisson solution: the well stays square (flat-band).
